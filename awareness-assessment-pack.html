PORTFOLIO ARTEFACT 04 • KNOWLEDGE & BEHAVIOUR

Employee Security Awareness Assessment Pack

A reusable pre/post-training assessment framework for measuring phishing recognition, verification decisions, credential protection and incident-reporting knowledge. Any demonstration results shown in this portfolio are synthetic and are not real employee performance data.

PRE-TRAINING

Baseline Knowledge Check

  1. An email from a senior executive asks for an urgent confidential payment. What should you do first?
  2. A login warning contains a link. How should you verify whether the warning is genuine?
  3. Why is MFA valuable even when a password is strong?
  4. Name three indicators that can make an email suspicious.
  5. What should you do after accidentally clicking a suspicious link?
POST-TRAINING

Applied Decision Check

  1. A supplier emails new bank details. Describe the independent verification process you should follow.
  2. An HR-themed attachment arrives unexpectedly. What checks should occur before opening it?
  3. Explain STOP → INSPECT → VERIFY → REPORT in your own words.
  4. Why should suspected phishing be reported even when you did not click?
  5. What is wrong with using only the sender display name as proof of identity?

SCORING MODEL

Portfolio measurement framework

Knowledge ScorePercentage of assessment items answered correctly before and after training.
Recognition ScoreAbility to identify phishing/social-engineering warning signs in simulated examples.
Verification ScoreAbility to choose a trusted independent verification channel rather than replying to the suspicious message.
Reporting ScoreUnderstanding of when and how to report suspicious activity or an accidental interaction.

SYNTHETIC DEMONSTRATION

Example programme results

61%Baseline knowledge
89%Post-training knowledge
+28Percentage-point improvement
72%Example reporting readiness

Interpretation

In this synthetic example, post-training knowledge improves materially, but reporting readiness remains lower than knowledge performance. The programme response would therefore emphasise reporting practice, simple reporting channels and repeated scenario-based reinforcement rather than assuming that knowledge alone equals secure behaviour.
