# Project 01 — Enterprise Cybersecurity Risk Management

> **Portfolio case study:** Educational/simulated GRC assessment demonstrating a structured enterprise cyber-risk workflow. This is not a production audit or assurance engagement.

## Executive Summary
This project demonstrates an enterprise cybersecurity risk-management assessment from asset identification through risk treatment. The objective was to translate technical threats and vulnerabilities into defensible business-risk statements, apply consistent likelihood and impact scoring, prioritize material exposures, and recommend proportionate controls.

## Scenario
A simulated organisation required a repeatable method for identifying critical information assets, evaluating cyber threats and vulnerabilities, documenting risk ownership, and prioritising remediation according to business impact.

## Objectives
- Establish a risk-based view of critical assets and dependencies.
- Document threat, vulnerability, likelihood, impact and risk rationale.
- Prioritise risks using a consistent scoring methodology.
- Recommend preventive, detective and corrective controls.
- Produce evidence suitable for management review and risk treatment.

## Environment / Tools
Educational GRC simulation, asset inventory, risk register, likelihood-impact matrix, control assessment and documented evidence.

## Methodology
1. Identified and classified relevant assets.
2. Mapped plausible threats and vulnerabilities to those assets.
3. Assessed likelihood and business impact using the simulation's risk matrix.
4. Recorded inherent risk and supporting rationale.
5. Reviewed existing controls and residual exposure where applicable.
6. Proposed risk-treatment actions and accountable ownership.

## Investigation or Assessment
The assessment focused on whether risk statements were specific enough to support decision-making, whether scoring was internally consistent, and whether controls addressed the underlying threat/vulnerability pathway rather than merely documenting the risk.

## Key Findings
Material cyber risks were concentrated around critical assets, privileged access, data exposure, service availability and control effectiveness. The exercise demonstrated that asset criticality alone does not determine risk: likelihood, business impact, exposure and control maturity must be considered together.

## Risk / Security Analysis
Risks were expressed as cause-event-impact relationships and prioritised according to the assessment matrix. High and Critical risks were treated as management priorities requiring stronger control coverage, defined ownership and measurable remediation rather than informal acceptance.

## Controls and Recommendations
Recommended measures include stronger identity and access management, MFA for privileged and remote access, least privilege, vulnerability and patch management, centralised logging and monitoring, tested backups, incident-response procedures, security awareness and periodic control testing. Risk treatment should be tracked to closure with accountable owners and target dates.

## Evidence
The repository evidence for this case study should be read as training evidence showing the assessment workflow, completed fields, scoring rationale and control decisions. Screenshots do not represent production assurance evidence.

## Skills Demonstrated
Cyber risk assessment; asset classification; risk-register development; likelihood-impact scoring; control analysis; risk treatment; governance documentation; business-impact communication.

## Frameworks / Standards Referenced
- NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0
- NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1 — Guide for Conducting Risk Assessments
- ISO/IEC 27001:2022 and ISO/IEC 27005:2022
- CIS Critical Security Controls v8.1

## Lessons Learned
A useful risk register must explain why a risk matters, not simply assign a colour or score. Strong GRC work links assets, threats, vulnerabilities, impacts, controls, ownership and treatment decisions in a traceable chain.

## Disclaimer
This portfolio project is based on an educational/simulated environment. Organisation names, assets, risks and findings are fictional or training-derived. No claim is made that a real organisation was audited, certified, penetrated or assured.
