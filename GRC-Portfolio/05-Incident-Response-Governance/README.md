# Project 05 — Cybersecurity Incident Response Governance Assessment

> **Portfolio case study:** Educational/simulated incident-response governance exercise demonstrating preparation, escalation, evidence and improvement practices.

## Executive Summary
This project evaluates organisational readiness to govern cybersecurity incidents from detection and triage through containment, recovery and post-incident improvement. It emphasises roles, escalation, communications, evidence preservation and decision accountability alongside technical response.

## Scenario
A simulated organisation needs a repeatable incident-response governance model capable of coordinating security, IT, management and other stakeholders during a material cyber incident.

## Objectives
- Assess incident-response roles, procedures and escalation paths.
- Evaluate detection-to-response governance and evidence handling.
- Identify communication and decision-making gaps.
- Recommend improvements for containment, recovery and lessons learned.

## Environment / Tools
Educational GRC simulation, incident-response plan, severity matrix, escalation workflow, evidence records, communication procedures and control assessment.

## Methodology
1. Reviewed incident roles and preparation requirements.
2. Assessed identification, triage and severity classification.
3. Evaluated escalation, containment and recovery governance.
4. Reviewed evidence preservation and communication expectations.
5. Documented gaps and recommended corrective actions.

## Investigation or Assessment
The exercise considered whether responsibilities were unambiguous, high-severity events could be escalated quickly, evidence could be preserved, decisions could be reconstructed after the event and recovery would include validation rather than simply restoring service.

## Key Findings
Incident response is weakened when ownership, severity criteria, communications or recovery authority are unclear. Technical detection alone does not ensure effective response; governance determines whether the right people act at the right time with traceable decisions.

## Risk / Security Analysis
Delayed escalation, incomplete evidence and untested response procedures increase operational, legal, regulatory and reputational impact. Governance controls therefore reduce both incident duration and secondary consequences.

## Controls and Recommendations
Maintain and exercise an incident-response plan; define severity and escalation criteria; maintain contact trees; establish evidence-handling procedures; preserve centralised logs; create containment playbooks; define crisis communications; conduct tabletop exercises; validate recovery; and track post-incident corrective actions.

## Evidence
Evidence should demonstrate the simulated incident workflow, governance decisions, completed assessment fields and recommended improvements. It is educational evidence, not documentation of a real security incident.

## Skills Demonstrated
Incident-response governance; severity assessment; escalation design; evidence handling; stakeholder coordination; business-impact analysis; remediation planning; lessons-learned process.

## Frameworks / Standards Referenced
- NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0
- NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3 — Incident Response Recommendations and Considerations for Cybersecurity Risk Management
- ISO/IEC 27001:2022
- CIS Critical Security Controls v8.1

## Lessons Learned
Effective incident response depends on preparation and decision discipline. Clear ownership, tested procedures and reliable evidence make technical response faster and management decisions more defensible.

## Disclaimer
This project is an educational/simulated incident-response governance exercise. It does not describe or disclose a real organisation's incident, breach or internal response process.
