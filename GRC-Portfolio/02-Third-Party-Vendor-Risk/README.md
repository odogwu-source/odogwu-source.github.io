# Project 02 — Third-Party & Vendor Cyber Risk Assessment

> **Portfolio case study:** Educational/simulated third-party risk assessment. It demonstrates methodology and judgement; it is not a real supplier audit or certification.

## Executive Summary
This project assesses cybersecurity risk introduced by third-party suppliers and service dependencies. The work demonstrates how vendor criticality, data access, service dependency, security controls and contractual obligations can be converted into risk-based due-diligence findings and remediation priorities.

## Scenario
A simulated organisation relies on external providers that may process sensitive information, support critical services or receive privileged/system access. The assessment evaluates whether those relationships introduce unacceptable cyber, privacy, operational or resilience exposure.

## Objectives
- Classify suppliers according to business and information-security criticality.
- Identify vendor-related threats, vulnerabilities and concentration/dependency risk.
- Assess security-control maturity and evidence gaps.
- Prioritise remediation and contractual safeguards.
- Define ongoing monitoring and reassessment requirements.

## Environment / Tools
Educational GRC simulation, vendor inventory, due-diligence questionnaire, risk matrix, control review, evidence register and remediation tracking.

## Methodology
1. Identified vendor services, data access and system dependencies.
2. Determined inherent risk based on criticality and exposure.
3. Reviewed available security-control evidence.
4. Documented gaps and scored resulting risk.
5. Proposed treatment, contractual and monitoring actions.

## Investigation or Assessment
Assessment emphasis was placed on access scope, sensitive-data handling, incident notification, resilience, subcontractor dependency, assurance evidence and the difference between vendor claims and independently supportable evidence.

## Key Findings
Third-party exposure can become enterprise exposure when suppliers handle sensitive data or support critical operations. Missing assurance evidence, excessive access, weak incident-notification provisions and insufficient continuity arrangements materially increase residual risk.

## Risk / Security Analysis
Vendor risk was evaluated as a combination of inherent exposure and control effectiveness. Critical suppliers require deeper due diligence and stronger ongoing oversight than low-impact vendors; a questionnaire alone is not sufficient assurance.

## Controls and Recommendations
Use risk-tiered vendor due diligence; security and privacy clauses; breach-notification requirements; least-privilege access; MFA; encryption; defined RTO/RPO where relevant; right-to-audit/assurance provisions; evidence-based onboarding; periodic reassessment; offboarding controls; and continuous monitoring for critical suppliers.

## Evidence
Evidence should demonstrate vendor classification, completed assessment fields, risk scoring, identified control gaps and treatment decisions. All artefacts are educational/simulated evidence rather than production supplier assurance.

## Skills Demonstrated
Third-party risk management; vendor due diligence; inherent/residual risk analysis; control evaluation; evidence review; contractual security requirements; remediation prioritisation; stakeholder communication.

## Frameworks / Standards Referenced
- NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0 — Govern / Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management
- NIST SP 800-161 Rev. 1 — Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management Practices
- ISO/IEC 27001:2022 — supplier relationship controls
- CIS Critical Security Controls v8.1

## Lessons Learned
Supplier risk cannot be outsourced with the service. Effective third-party risk management requires evidence, proportional oversight, clear contractual obligations and reassessment throughout the relationship lifecycle.

## Disclaimer
This is an educational/simulated portfolio case study. Supplier identities, findings and risk ratings are fictional or training-derived and must not be interpreted as assessments of real organisations.
