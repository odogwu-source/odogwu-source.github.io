# Project 04 — IT Readiness & Compliance Gap Assessment

> **Portfolio case study:** Educational/simulated compliance-readiness assessment. Framework references are used for control mapping and learning; they do not constitute certification or formal compliance attestation.

## Executive Summary
This project demonstrates a structured readiness assessment used to identify gaps between current security practices and target governance/control requirements. The work focuses on evidence quality, control design, implementation status, ownership and remediation rather than treating compliance as a checklist exercise.

## Scenario
A simulated organisation is preparing for stronger cybersecurity governance and needs to understand whether documented policies, technical safeguards, operational procedures and supporting evidence are sufficient for a future formal assessment.

## Objectives
- Establish an evidence-based view of control readiness.
- Identify missing, partially implemented or ineffective controls.
- Map gaps to recognised cybersecurity requirements.
- Prioritise remediation according to risk and business impact.
- Produce an auditable remediation roadmap.

## Environment / Tools
Educational GRC simulation, control checklist, evidence register, policy/procedure review, gap tracker, risk matrix and remediation plan.

## Methodology
1. Defined the target control requirements.
2. Reviewed available policy, process and technical evidence.
3. Classified control status based on supportable evidence.
4. Documented gaps and associated risks.
5. Assigned remediation priorities, owners and recommended actions.

## Investigation or Assessment
The assessment distinguished between a documented control, an implemented control and an effective control. Evidence sufficiency was evaluated alongside ownership, repeatability and whether the control could be independently demonstrated.

## Key Findings
Readiness gaps commonly arise where policies exist without operational evidence, controls are implemented without formal ownership, or technical safeguards are present but not periodically tested. Such gaps reduce both security assurance and auditability.

## Risk / Security Analysis
Compliance gaps were prioritised according to the underlying cybersecurity exposure rather than document completeness alone. Higher-risk gaps require accelerated treatment, while lower-risk administrative improvements can be incorporated into a structured remediation programme.

## Controls and Recommendations
Maintain a control/evidence matrix; assign accountable owners; establish policy review cycles; collect durable evidence; implement access reviews; strengthen vulnerability and patch governance; formalise incident response and business continuity; perform periodic control testing; and track corrective actions through closure.

## Evidence
Evidence should show completed control assessments, documented gaps, risk prioritisation and remediation decisions. Portfolio artefacts are training outputs and do not represent an audit opinion or compliance certification.

## Skills Demonstrated
Compliance readiness; control-gap analysis; evidence evaluation; policy/process review; control mapping; remediation planning; audit-readiness thinking; governance documentation.

## Frameworks / Standards Referenced
- NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0
- ISO/IEC 27001:2022
- CIS Critical Security Controls v8.1
- COBIT 2019 governance and management objectives

## Lessons Learned
Compliance evidence must demonstrate that a control operates, not merely that someone wrote it down. Readiness work becomes more valuable when gaps are connected to actual cyber risk and assigned measurable remediation actions.

## Disclaimer
This is an educational/simulated readiness assessment. Framework mapping is illustrative and does not imply certification, legal advice or formal assurance against any standard.
