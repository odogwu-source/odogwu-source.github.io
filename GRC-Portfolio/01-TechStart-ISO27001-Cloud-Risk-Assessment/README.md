# TechStart — ISO 27001 Cloud Risk Assessment

## Executive Summary
This educational GRC simulation demonstrates a structured cloud-security risk assessment using ISO/IEC 27001 risk-management concepts. The project connects asset and threat identification with likelihood/impact analysis, risk ownership, treatment decisions, control selection and evidence retention. The emphasis is on producing a defensible risk-assessment trail rather than treating compliance as a checklist.

## Scenario
TechStart operates cloud-hosted technology services that depend on the confidentiality, integrity and availability of business information and supporting systems. The simulated engagement required the identification, evaluation and treatment of information-security risks affecting the cloud environment.

## Objectives
- Establish a repeatable cloud risk-assessment workflow.
- Identify relevant information-security risks and affected assets.
- Evaluate likelihood and business impact.
- Prioritise risks using documented ratings.
- Assign risk ownership and treatment actions.
- Connect risk decisions to relevant security controls.
- Preserve an evidence trail suitable for governance and audit review.

## Environment / Tools
- Educational GRC practice environment
- Risk register and asset inventory workflow
- Likelihood × impact scoring
- Control mapping and treatment documentation
- Screenshot-based evidence capture

## Methodology
The assessment followed a practical risk lifecycle: establish context, identify assets and threats, analyse likelihood and impact, evaluate risk severity, select treatment actions, assign accountable ownership, document relevant controls and retain evidence for review.

## Investigation or Assessment
The exercise focused on cloud and information-security exposures that could affect business operations. Risks were documented in a structured register, scored according to likelihood and impact, assigned to owners and linked to treatment actions. Control decisions were recorded to demonstrate how risk analysis drives security governance.

## Key Findings
- Cloud risk should be prioritised according to business consequence rather than technical severity alone.
- Risk ownership is necessary to convert identified exposure into accountable action.
- Evidence and decision rationale materially strengthen the defensibility of risk ratings.
- Control selection is most useful when explicitly linked to an identified risk and expected treatment outcome.

## Risk / Security Analysis
The project applied qualitative risk analysis using likelihood and impact ratings. Risks with greater potential to disrupt critical services, expose information or undermine business objectives received higher priority. The exercise also demonstrated the distinction between identifying a threat and documenting the business risk created when that threat affects a vulnerable or critical asset.

## Controls and Recommendations
- Maintain a formal cloud risk register with named owners and review dates.
- Apply least privilege and strong authentication to cloud administration.
- Protect sensitive information through appropriate access, encryption and backup controls.
- Review cloud configurations and control effectiveness periodically.
- Track treatment actions to closure and reassess residual risk.
- Retain evidence supporting risk ratings and control decisions.

## Project Outcomes
The completed assessment produced a traceable risk-management workflow linking cloud risks, business impact, accountable ownership, treatment decisions and supporting evidence.

## Lessons Learned
A defensible cloud risk assessment is not simply a list of technical weaknesses. It explains why the risk matters to the organisation, who owns it, how it should be treated and what evidence supports the decision.

## Disclaimer
This project was completed in an educational/practice simulation environment for cybersecurity skills development and portfolio demonstration. It is not a production compliance assessment, certification, audit opinion or client engagement.
