# HealthTech Systems — Incident Response & GRC

## Executive Summary
This educational GRC simulation models incident-response governance for a healthcare technology environment. The project links critical-asset identification, incident documentation, severity assessment, control mapping, evidence preservation, continuity and recovery activities to ISO/IEC 27001:2022 control concepts.

## Assessment Snapshot
- **Principal incident scenarios:** 3
- **Scenarios:** ransomware, unauthorised patient-data access, phishing/account compromise
- **Focus:** governance, severity, ownership, evidence, continuity and recovery
- **Framework:** ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A concepts
- **Evidence:** retained in the project `screenshots/` directory

## Scenario
HealthTech Systems depends on critical patient-care technology and sensitive health information. The simulated environment required identification of critical assets, documentation of realistic cyber incidents and implementation of governance controls covering preparation, detection, response, evidence preservation, recovery and lessons learned.

## Objectives
- Identify and classify critical healthcare systems and data assets.
- Document ransomware, phishing/account-compromise and data-breach scenarios.
- Assign incident severity, status and accountable response owners.
- Map incident-response activities to relevant ISO/IEC 27001:2022 controls.
- Preserve an evidence trail supporting governance and audit readiness.

## Environment / Tools
- Educational GRC practice environment
- Asset inventory and incident register
- Incident severity and ownership workflow
- ISO/IEC 27001 control mapping
- Screenshot-based evidence capture

## Methodology
The exercise followed an incident-governance lifecycle: identify critical assets, document the incident scenario, classify severity, assign ownership, define response and evidence requirements, map controls, support continuity/recovery and capture post-incident learning.

## Assessment
Three principal scenarios were documented: **ransomware disrupting critical patient systems, unauthorised access exposing patient information, and phishing leading to employee-account compromise**. The assessment considered how governance should support response to each scenario and how business/clinical impact should influence prioritisation.

## Key Findings
- Incident-response governance depends on accurate critical-asset awareness and clearly assigned ownership.
- Evidence preservation and logging are necessary for defensible investigation and post-incident review.
- Healthcare incidents can create simultaneous confidentiality, integrity, availability, operational and patient-safety impacts.
- Continuity and backup controls are components of incident preparedness, not separate administrative activities.
- Severity decisions should reflect business and service consequences as well as technical indicators.

## Risk / Security Analysis
The simulated incidents demonstrated different risk patterns. Ransomware primarily threatened service availability and operational resilience; unauthorised patient-data access created confidentiality and regulatory exposure; phishing/account compromise introduced identity, lateral-access and further-compromise risk. Response priorities therefore needed to reflect both technical and organisational consequences.

## Controls and Recommendations
- Maintain documented incident-response preparation and decision processes.
- Monitor security events and escalate according to defined criteria.
- Define containment, eradication and recovery responsibilities.
- Preserve incident evidence in a controlled and traceable manner.
- Maintain tested backups and continuity procedures for critical healthcare systems.
- Maintain reliable security logging to support investigation and evidence.
- Conduct post-incident reviews and feed lessons learned into control improvements.

## Project Outcomes
The project produced a structured incident-governance case study linking critical healthcare assets, three distinct incident scenarios, severity, ownership, control mapping, evidence, continuity and lessons learned.

## Lessons Learned
Effective incident-response governance is more than reacting to alerts. It requires critical-asset awareness, defined ownership, repeatable procedures, defensible evidence, continuity planning and a feedback loop that converts incident experience into stronger controls.

## Disclaimer
This project was completed in an educational/practice simulation environment for cybersecurity skills development and portfolio demonstration. The scenarios and controls are used for training and are not presented as a production healthcare assessment, formal compliance opinion or client engagement.
