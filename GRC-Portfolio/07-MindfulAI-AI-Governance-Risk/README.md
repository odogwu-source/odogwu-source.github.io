# Project 07 — AI Governance & Responsible AI Risk Assessment

## Executive Summary
This hands-on GRC simulation assessed governance and security risks across five production AI systems spanning healthcare, financial services, generative AI, recruitment and fraud/security analytics. The work established an AI-system inventory, assessed six material AI risks, documented governance controls and created AI policies. The project demonstrates responsible-AI governance across bias, privacy, model drift, prompt injection, explainability, human oversight, monitoring and incident response.

## Scenario
MindfulAI Labs operates multiple AI-enabled services with different levels of business and societal impact. The simulated engagement required a governance structure capable of identifying AI assets, assessing material risks, assigning accountability, documenting controls and establishing policies for responsible AI use.

## Objectives
- Inventory and classify five production AI systems.
- Assess six AI governance and security risks.
- Document ten AI governance controls.
- Establish four supporting AI policies.
- Demonstrate accountable risk ownership and evidence-based risk scoring.
- Connect AI governance requirements to operational security and monitoring.

## Environment / Tools
- Educational GRC simulation platform
- AI asset inventory and classification
- Risk register and 5x5 likelihood-impact scoring
- NIST Cybersecurity Framework governance/control references
- Responsible AI and model-risk concepts
- Security monitoring, IAM and incident-response concepts

## Methodology
1. Identified AI systems, owners, environment and criticality.
2. Evaluated each system's data, decision impact and attack surface.
3. Developed AI-specific risk scenarios.
4. Scored likelihood and impact and documented decision rationale.
5. Designed governance, security, monitoring and oversight controls.
6. Assessed control effectiveness rather than assuming universal compliance.
7. Established policies covering responsible use, privacy, model risk/security and incident response/human oversight.

## Investigation or Assessment
The inventory included an AI Medical Diagnostic Support System, AI Credit Risk Scoring System, Generative AI Customer Support Assistant, AI Recruitment Screening System, and AI Fraud & Security Anomaly Detection System. The risk assessment covered algorithmic bias/discrimination, sensitive-data exposure, model drift and inaccurate decisions, generative-AI prompt injection, inadequate explainability/transparency, and insufficient human oversight of high-impact decisions.

## Key Findings
- AI model bias causing discriminatory outcomes was rated **20/25 — Critical**.
- Sensitive personal-data exposure was rated **20/25 — Critical**.
- Model drift and inaccurate decisions were rated **16/25 — High**.
- Prompt injection affecting generative AI was rated **16/25 — High**.
- Explainability/transparency limitations were rated **12/25 — High**.
- Inadequate human oversight of high-impact AI decisions was rated **15/25 — High**.
- Fairness/model-performance monitoring and explainability were treated as areas requiring further standardization rather than being marked universally effective.

## Risk / Security Analysis
The assessment showed that AI risk extends beyond conventional confidentiality, integrity and availability. High-impact AI systems introduce fairness, explainability, model-performance and human-oversight concerns, while generative AI introduces additional manipulation and sensitive-data exposure risks. Risk treatment therefore requires governance, technical security, lifecycle monitoring and accountable human decision-making.

## Controls and Recommendations
Controls addressed AI governance/accountability, AI inventory and classification, access control, sensitive-data protection, AI/model vulnerability management, security logging and monitoring, bias/fairness and model-performance monitoring, human oversight, transparency/explainability documentation, and AI incident response/model containment. Recommendations include standardized fairness metrics, documented drift thresholds, stronger explainability requirements for high-impact use cases, continuous security monitoring, formal human override paths, privacy-by-design, and periodic governance review.

## Project Outcomes
Produced an evidence-backed AI governance case study linking diverse AI systems to risk scenarios, quantified risk decisions, governance controls and policy requirements. The project demonstrates the ability to translate emerging AI risks into practical GRC actions.

## Lessons Learned
AI governance is a lifecycle discipline. Inventory and initial approval are not sufficient: models require ongoing monitoring for drift, bias, security abuse and changing business impact, while high-impact decisions require transparent documentation and meaningful human oversight.

## Disclaimer
This project was completed in a simulated educational environment for cybersecurity training and portfolio demonstration. The organisation, AI systems, risks and assessment activities do not represent a production engagement with a real client.
