# EuroShop — GDPR Compliance

## Executive Summary
This educational GRC simulation demonstrates privacy governance and GDPR-oriented compliance work in an e-commerce environment processing personal data. The project focuses on mapping data-processing activity to privacy risk, accountability, safeguards, remediation and retained evidence rather than presenting GDPR as a checklist exercise.

## Assessment Snapshot
- **Scenario:** e-commerce personal-data processing
- **Focus:** privacy governance, accountability and control-gap analysis
- **Evidence:** 20 captured screenshots
- **Core principles:** purpose limitation, data minimisation, storage limitation, security and accountability
- **Status:** educational simulation; not a formal legal or regulatory assessment

## Scenario
EuroShop processes customer and business information through e-commerce operations. The simulated engagement required review of personal-data processing, associated privacy risks, accountability responsibilities, safeguards and remediation needs.

## Objectives
- Identify personal-data processing activities and privacy exposure.
- Apply GDPR governance principles to an e-commerce scenario.
- Document processing purposes, responsibilities and control requirements.
- Identify privacy/control gaps and remediation actions.
- Preserve evidence supporting accountability and review.

## Environment / Tools
- Educational GRC practice environment
- Privacy and data-processing assessment workflow
- Compliance-gap and remediation tracking
- Governance documentation
- Screenshot-based evidence capture

## Methodology
The assessment followed a data-lifecycle perspective: identify what personal data is processed, why it is processed, where it flows, who can access it, how long it is retained, what safeguards apply and how rights/incident obligations are supported. The analysis then translated those observations into governance actions and evidence requirements.

## Assessment
The project examined simulated processing activities for privacy exposure and accountability requirements. Risks and control gaps were documented, ownership was considered and remediation actions were identified to strengthen governance over personal information.

## Key Findings
- Effective privacy governance depends on visibility into processing activities, purposes and data flows.
- Accountability requires defined responsibility and evidence showing that safeguards are implemented.
- Excessive collection, access or retention increases privacy exposure.
- Purpose, minimisation and retention decisions should be documented and periodically reviewed.
- Privacy controls are stronger when embedded into operational processes rather than treated as a one-time compliance exercise.

## Risk / Security Analysis
The assessment considered the potential impact of inappropriate access, excessive processing, poor retention practices, weak governance or insufficient incident readiness. Privacy risk was evaluated in terms of effects on individuals as well as legal, regulatory, operational and reputational consequences for the organisation.

## Controls and Recommendations
- Maintain accurate records of relevant personal-data processing activities.
- Define and document processing purposes and applicable governance responsibilities.
- Apply data minimisation so collected information is adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary.
- Restrict access according to business need and least privilege.
- Define retention periods and secure-disposal requirements rather than retaining data indefinitely.
- Maintain processes supporting individual rights and personal-data incident response.
- Review privacy controls periodically and retain evidence demonstrating accountability.

## Lessons Learned
Privacy compliance requires demonstrable accountability. Organisations need to understand why personal data is used, limit processing to legitimate purposes, avoid unnecessary collection and retention, apply proportionate safeguards and retain evidence that privacy obligations are embedded in day-to-day operations.
