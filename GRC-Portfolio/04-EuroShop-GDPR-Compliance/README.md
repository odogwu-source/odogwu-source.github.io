# EuroShop — GDPR Compliance

## Executive Summary
This educational GRC simulation demonstrates privacy governance and GDPR-oriented compliance activities in an e-commerce environment processing personal data. The project focuses on understanding data-processing risk, documenting accountability, identifying control gaps and translating privacy obligations into practical governance actions.

## Scenario
EuroShop processes customer and business information through e-commerce operations. The simulated engagement required review of personal-data processing, associated privacy risks, accountability responsibilities, safeguards and remediation needs.

## Objectives
- Identify personal-data processing activities and privacy exposure.
- Apply GDPR governance principles to an e-commerce scenario.
- Document compliance responsibilities and control requirements.
- Identify gaps and remediation actions.
- Preserve evidence for accountability and review.

## Environment / Tools
- Educational GRC practice environment
- Privacy and data-processing assessment workflow
- Compliance-gap and remediation tracking
- Governance documentation
- Screenshot-based evidence capture

## Methodology
The assessment followed a data-lifecycle perspective: understand what personal data is processed, why it is processed, where it flows, who can access it, how long it is retained, what safeguards apply and how data-subject and incident-response obligations are supported.

## Investigation or Assessment
The project examined simulated processing activities for privacy exposure and accountability requirements. Risks and control gaps were documented, ownership was assigned and remediation actions were identified to strengthen governance over personal information.

## Key Findings
- Effective privacy governance depends on visibility into data-processing activities and data flows.
- Accountability requires defined owners, documented purposes and evidence of safeguards.
- Excessive collection, access or retention can increase privacy risk.
- Privacy controls should be embedded into operational processes rather than treated as a one-time compliance exercise.

## Risk / Security Analysis
The assessment considered the potential impact of inappropriate access, excessive processing, poor retention practices, weak governance or insufficient incident readiness. Privacy risk was evaluated in terms of the potential effect on individuals as well as legal, regulatory, operational and reputational consequences for the organisation.

## Controls and Recommendations
- Maintain an accurate record of personal-data processing activities.
- Apply data minimisation and purpose-limitation principles.
- Restrict access according to business need and least privilege.
- Define retention and secure-disposal requirements.
- Establish processes supporting data-subject rights and incident response.
- Review privacy controls periodically and retain evidence of accountability.

## Project Outcomes
The project produced a structured privacy-governance workflow linking processing activities, privacy exposure, accountability, safeguards, control gaps and remediation actions.

## Lessons Learned
Privacy compliance requires demonstrable accountability. Organisations need to understand how personal data is used, limit processing to legitimate purposes, apply proportionate safeguards and retain evidence that privacy obligations are embedded in day-to-day operations.

## Disclaimer
This project was completed in an educational/practice simulation environment for cybersecurity skills development and portfolio demonstration. It is not legal advice, a formal GDPR compliance determination, a regulatory assessment or a production client engagement.
