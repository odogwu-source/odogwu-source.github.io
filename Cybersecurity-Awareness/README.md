# Cybersecurity Awareness & Human-Risk Portfolio

A practical, simulated security-awareness programme demonstrating how cybersecurity education can reduce human risk through realistic scenarios, clear employee guidance, assessment, measurement, management reporting and continuous improvement.

> **Portfolio integrity:** The organisational scenario and example results are educational/simulated artefacts. Synthetic metrics are clearly labelled and are not presented as results from real employees, clients or production engagements.

## Recruiter Quick Start

This portfolio demonstrates a complete awareness lifecycle rather than a single training presentation:

**Human-risk identification → Audience and learning objectives → Training design → Safe simulation → Knowledge/behaviour assessment → Measurement → Management reporting → Continuous improvement**

## Methodology

The programme treats awareness as a **risk-management and behaviour-change function**, not simply a completion exercise. Learning objectives are tied to observable employee decisions such as recognising suspicious requests, verifying through trusted channels, protecting credentials, reporting suspected phishing and responding appropriately after an error.

The measurement model intentionally uses more than a single click-rate metric. Scenario context and difficulty can influence user performance, so interpretation should consider the human element as well as raw interaction rates. The portfolio therefore combines knowledge measures, reporting behaviour, interaction indicators, scenario context and management-level trend analysis.

## Professional Value

The programme demonstrates the ability to translate technical cybersecurity threats into guidance that non-technical employees can understand and act on. It connects training to measurable outcomes and management reporting, helping position security awareness as part of organisational risk management rather than a compliance-only exercise.

## Framework Alignment

The portfolio methodology is consistent with the principles in **NIST SP 800-50 Rev. 1, Building a Cybersecurity and Privacy Learning Program**, particularly lifecycle-based programme management, behaviour change, role/audience relevance, measurement and continuous improvement. The phishing measurement approach also reflects the human-centred principle behind the **NIST Phish Scale**: phishing exercise outcomes should be interpreted in context rather than using click rate alone as a complete measure of employee capability.

These references provide methodological alignment only; this portfolio does **not** claim NIST certification or formal organisational implementation.

## Evidence & Ethics Statement

All example organisations, scenarios and results are fictional or training-derived. Metrics displayed in the dashboard are **synthetic demonstration data**. The materials demonstrate programme design, communication, measurement and reporting capability; they do not claim that a real organisation or workforce was assessed or trained.

Future authorised organisational exercises should be documented separately, with appropriate permission, privacy controls and anonymisation, so real evidence remains distinguishable from portfolio simulation data.
