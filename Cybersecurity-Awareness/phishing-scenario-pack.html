PORTFOLIO ARTEFACT 03 • SAFE SIMULATION

Simulated Phishing Scenario Pack

Four fictional, non-operational phishing-awareness scenarios designed for employee education. They use reserved example domains, contain no credential-collection mechanism, and are not intended for deployment against real people.

Assessment approach

Each scenario documents the business context, social-engineering trigger, observable warning signs, expected employee response and an illustrative human-detection difficulty rating. The rating is a portfolio teaching aid inspired by NIST's principle that phishing results should be interpreted in the context of message cues and user context; it is not represented as an official NIST Phish Scale score.

SAFE • SYNTHETIC • EDUCATIONAL
SCENARIO 01EXECUTIVE IMPERSONATION

Urgent Finance Transfer Request

Fictional message: “I’m entering a meeting. Please arrange the confidential supplier payment today and reply when done.”

Context: Finance employee receives an unusual high-pressure request appearing to come from senior leadership.

  • Urgency and secrecy discourage verification
  • Unexpected payment request outside normal workflow
  • Sender identity should be independently verified
  • Financial process must never be bypassed because of authority pressure

Expected response: Do not transfer funds. Verify through a known internal channel and report the message.

ILLUSTRATIVE DIFFICULTY: MODERATE–HIGH
SCENARIO 02CREDENTIAL LURE

Cloud Account Expiry Notice

Fictional message: “Your corporate cloud account expires today. Confirm access at login-security.example.”

Context: General employee receives a security-themed login warning.

  • Artificial deadline creates urgency
  • Link domain does not match the organisation's real service
  • Unexpected credential request
  • Security language is used to make the lure appear trustworthy

Expected response: Do not use the link. Open the service through a known bookmark/app, verify status, and report the email.

ILLUSTRATIVE DIFFICULTY: MODERATE
SCENARIO 03HR PRETEXT

Updated Benefits Document

Fictional message: “HR has updated your benefits package. Review the attached document before 4 PM.”

Context: Employees receive a message aligned with a plausible workplace topic.

  • Work-relevant pretext may lower suspicion
  • Unexpected attachment should be treated cautiously
  • Deadline encourages fast action
  • Employee should confirm the announcement through the HR portal or known HR contact

Expected response: Do not open an unexpected attachment until independently verified; report if suspicious.

ILLUSTRATIVE DIFFICULTY: HIGH
SCENARIO 04VENDOR / BEC

Supplier Bank Details Changed

Fictional message: “Our banking details have changed. Please use the new account for all outstanding invoices.”

Context: Accounts Payable receives a plausible request apparently from an established supplier.

  • Payment-detail change is a high-risk business event
  • Display name alone is not proof of identity
  • Replying to the same email does not independently verify the request
  • Known supplier contact and approved change-control process should be used

Expected response: Pause payment, verify via established supplier records, follow finance controls and report suspected impersonation.

ILLUSTRATIVE DIFFICULTY: HIGH

ANALYST VIEW

What these scenarios demonstrate

Human-factor analysisUrgency, authority, familiarity, fear and business relevance can influence employee decisions.
Context mattersA message that fits a recipient's normal work can be harder to detect than an obviously irrelevant lure.
Reporting is a positive security actionEmployees should have a simple, well-understood route to report suspicious messages quickly.
Metrics need contextClick/report rates should be interpreted alongside scenario difficulty and employee context, not in isolation.

Portfolio integrity & safety

All names, messages, links and results in this artefact are fictional. Reserved example domains are used to avoid directing users to real login infrastructure. No real credential capture, impersonation campaign or unsolicited phishing delivery is included.
