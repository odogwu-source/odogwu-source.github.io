COMPLETED ARTEFACT • EMPLOYEE-FACING SECURITY MATERIAL

Cybersecurity Awareness Materials Pack

A practical employee reference pack supporting phishing recognition, social-engineering defence, account protection and rapid incident reporting. This portfolio artefact is designed for use alongside the Employee Cybersecurity Awareness Training Deck.

EMPLOYEE DECISION MODEL

STOP → INSPECT → VERIFY → REPORT

STOP: Do not act immediately on unexpected or urgent requests. INSPECT: Check the sender, wording, link destination, attachment and request. VERIFY: Confirm unusual requests using a trusted communication channel, not the contact details supplied in the suspicious message. REPORT: Send suspicious activity through the organisation's approved reporting process promptly.

CORE BEHAVIOUR: PAUSE BEFORE TRUSTING THE MESSAGE

MATERIAL 01

Phishing & Social Engineering Quick-Reference Checklist

Check the senderLook for misspelled domains, unexpected external senders, display-name impersonation and unusual reply-to addresses.
Challenge urgencyBe suspicious of pressure to act immediately, bypass procedure, keep a request secret or make an unusual payment.
Inspect linksBefore following a link, inspect where it really leads. Treat shortened, mismatched or look-alike domains with caution.
Treat attachments carefullyDo not open unexpected files simply because the message appears to come from a known person or organisation.
Protect credentialsNever provide passwords or authentication codes because an email, text or caller asks for them. Use MFA and approved authentication processes.
Verify independentlyFor unusual financial, credential or sensitive-data requests, contact the person through a known number, directory entry or established internal channel.
Recognise multiple channelsSocial engineering can arrive by email, SMS, telephone, social media, collaboration tools or in person.
Report quicklyIf something feels wrong—or you already clicked, replied or disclosed information—report it immediately so the security team can respond.

MATERIAL 02

Awareness Poster — Think Before You Act

🛡️

AN URGENT MESSAGE IS NOT AN INSTRUCTION TO PANIC.

Attackers often exploit authority, urgency, fear, curiosity and trust. Before sending money, sharing data, opening an attachment, entering credentials or approving an MFA request, stop and verify.

Unexpected?Urgent?Asking for credentials?Payment change?Suspicious link?Unknown attachment?

When in doubt: do not interact with the suspicious content. Verify through a trusted channel and report it.

MATERIAL 03

Employee Incident-Reporting Guide

01DO NOT CONTINUE

Contain your actions

Stop interacting with the suspicious message or site. Do not send additional information, approve further prompts or continue the conversation.

02REPORT

Use the approved channel

Use the organisation's phishing-report button, service desk, SOC mailbox or incident-reporting process. Preserve the suspicious message where possible.

03EXPLAIN

Tell security what happened

State what you received, when you received it, what action you took, whether you clicked/opened/replied, and whether credentials, data or money may be involved.

04FOLLOW RESPONSE

Cooperate with containment

Follow security-team instructions. Depending on the incident this may include changing credentials, ending sessions, isolating a device or providing additional evidence.

MANAGER / TRAINER NOTES

How this pack supports a security culture

The materials emphasise recognition and rapid reporting, not punishment. Employees should be encouraged to report suspicious activity even after an accidental click or disclosure. Fast reporting gives defenders more time to investigate, contain and reduce organisational impact.
